# Certification Plan

The purpose of certifications here is to validate knowledge after it exists, not to substitute for it.

I would rather have two respected certifications backed by real projects than a page full of badges I cannot defend in an interview.

## Current candidates

### AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer — Associate

**When:** after the ML foundation plus meaningful hands-on ML/cloud/deployment work.

Why it is interesting: it aligns with ML engineering, deployment, operations and MLOps/LLMOps-oriented work.

**Status:** future candidate — not ready yet.

### Microsoft Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate (AI-103)

**When:** after the LLM-engineering phase, especially once I have built generative-AI, RAG/tool/agent systems and have Azure/Foundry practice.

Why it is interesting: application/agent-oriented AI engineering is close to the eventual LLM-engineering target.

**Status:** future candidate — reassess when prerequisites are present.

### Google Cloud Professional Machine Learning Engineer

**When:** later, if Google Cloud/Vertex AI becomes relevant to my projects or target roles.

This is a professional-level credential and should not be treated as an early-learning badge.

**Status:** optional future candidate.

## What does not need to become a certification

Course certificates (Udemy, DeepLearning.AI, Coursera, etc.) can still appear in the learning history/LinkedIn when useful, but they are different from independently assessed industry certifications.

Do not spend months collecting introductory cloud/AI badges just because they exist. Add one only when it fills a real gap or has clear career value.

## Before booking any exam

Because AI certifications change quickly, re-check:

- whether the certification still exists;
- the current exam code/version;
- current official objectives;
- prerequisites/recommended experience;
- market relevance to the roles I actually want;
- whether my projects already exercise the skills being tested.

The certification list in this repository is therefore intentionally revisable.
